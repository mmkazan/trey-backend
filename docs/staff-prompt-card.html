One line. Ten seconds. More reviews.

“If you were happy today — would you mind giving that a tap? Takes ten seconds.”

  • Ask as they pay, while they're still in front of you
  • Only ask people who seem pleased
  • Point at the stand — don't explain it, they'll work it out
  • If they say no, that's fine. Move on.
Every tap is a review that wouldn't have happened.

Out on a job? Same line, keyring instead.

“All done — if you were happy with it, would you mind tapping this? Two seconds.”

  • Ask when you're settling up, or over the brew
  • Hold the fob out — let them bring their phone to it
  • Phone not playing? Turn it over, point the camera at the QR
  • Older customers: “hold your phone near this”
Most of your work comes from Google. This is how you feed it.

Why we bother asking

One extra star on Google is worth 5–9% more revenue. That's the whole reason.

  • Nearly everyone checks reviews before choosing
  • Most happy customers would leave one — they just forget
  • The stand removes the faff. The asking is still down to us.
  • Ten asks a week beats one big push a month
Harvard Business School (Yelp study)
trey

Two things we never do

Never offer anything for a review. No discounts, no free coffee, no prize draw.

  • It's against Google's rules and can get reviews removed
  • Never ask only the happy ones on purpose — offer the tap to everyone you served well
  • Never leave a review for your own business
  • A bad review isn't a disaster — the owner replies to it, and that counts for more
Asking is fine. Paying for it isn't.
